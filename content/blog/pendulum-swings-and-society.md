I often use pendulum swings as an analogy for human behavior: both at the individual level as well as the societal level. Those of you more familiar with high school physics might recognize the resistance-less pendulum as a simple harmonic oscillator.

![A pendulum shown at five moments in one swing: centre at full speed, far right at rest, centre, far left at rest, and centre again. Beneath it, its displacement over time traces a sine wave between +A and −A.](/blog/pendulum/simple-harmonic.png)

Unfortunately for us, this model completely neglects resistance and the loss of energy to the environment. It also ignores the possibility of motivated actors actively influencing the pendulum to keep it moving in a particular way. Those of you familiar with university physics might recognize these concepts as damping and driving.

![A damped pendulum: each swing reaches less far than the last (84%, 59%, 42%, then 30% of the starting amplitude) while the period stays the same, its sine wave shrinking inside an exponential envelope.](/blog/pendulum/damped.png)

![A driven pendulum: starting from rest, a periodic drive force pushes it in step, and each swing reaches further (34%, 71%, 88%, then 95% of the steady-state amplitude) as it settles into the drive's rhythm.](/blog/pendulum/driven.png)

And yet, we have still only solved a very, very small subset of potential pendulum problems. See, this kind of nice sine curve (albeit a bit distorted) is a result of a crucial approximation we make when designing these oscillators: that the angles of displacement are very small. Of course, engineers are then careful to ENSURE that it’s nigh impossible for things utilizing the theory of these to go out of the bounds of the angles that the theories are reasonably well accounted for -- however, once we do allow for reality to take shape and for pendulums to begin at large angles, involving damping and driving, those of you familiar with more advanced physics courses might recognize the beginnings of chaos theory here.

![Two driven, damped pendulums started half a degree apart, at 160° and 160.5°. Their angles stay in step for about five swings, then diverge completely: by the end one is at −109° and the other at 22°.](/blog/pendulum/chaotic.png)

And for those of you who read “chaos theory” and infer “unpredictability” because like, who the hell knows the technical definition of chaos as it stands in physics, you are partially right -- but people specifically studying it have somehow managed to find “patterns in the chaos”, so to speak; often dubbed terms such as “period doubling cascades” and “fractals”.

![A bifurcation diagram of the pendulum's motion as the drive strength rises from 1.0 to 1.5: a single stable period, then period doubling, then scattered chaos, a clean periodic window, and chaos again.](/blog/pendulum/bifurcation.png)

Okay, well, enough with the physics nerdicism. Those of you familiar with philosophy or language might have read this far, connected it to the title and the opener, and know where this is going. The idea of representing society’s motions as pendulum swings has some gross oversimplifications; but the ideas of the physical pendulum ring analogously regardless.

See, damping is just people losing interest in caring about a cause or the set of behaviors. However, damping may also occur as a byproduct of just “time in the system” -- losing energy to environmental factors. When I often talk of swings as behaviour, I often mention people going from one end to the other, until they settle somewhere in the middle. Damping represents the loss of momentum in a movement as enthusiasm fades, resources diminish, or countervailing forces grow stronger. But the eventual equilibrium need not lie halfway between the original extremes; it depends on the forces acting on the system.

And external factors driving the pendulum? I couldn’t have been more on the nose with that one. When there are powerful factors (usually people) extremely motivated to keep a pendulum going for their own purposes that would have died down on its own, the pendulum eventually takes the shape of the rhythm of the driver. The classic ideology of powerful people forcing culture wars so that the oppressed people do not engage in class wars is basically a lay-up at this point.

And yet, what stands even stronger, is the chaos theoretical pendulum. This is a model that I would argue is the biggest advocate against reducing human behaviour into pendulum swings: If the radicalism of the bob becomes sufficiently pronounced, we simply cannot predict stable behaviour. Anything possible in its trajectory can happen. We just find patterns in the chaos and try to live in those patterns and survive through them.

And thus, I conclude, the pendulum a fitting analogy for human behaviour as a whole. However, not in the classical sense of the classic, uninterrupted, unbothered pendulum which approximates nicely to the case of society going left to right on matters every few decades then swinging back -- but rather the modern case of a reckless, ridiculous pendulum constantly affected by factors trying to drive it and factors trying to damp it. We must aim to find stability in the pendulums that converge nicely, and stay away from getting carried away in these evil chaotic ones lest we find ourselves at the verge of mental and societal breakdowns at constant time periods. However, I do find “progress in society”, as abstract and vague as that may be, to be a different kind of sine wave that is not exactly explainable by a pendulum (which would of course, imply that society always has a constant mean value for an attribute),

![An oscillating signal whose swings grow and shrink over time, wrapped around a dashed baseline that rises steadily from bottom left to top right.](/blog/pendulum/rising-baseline.png)

And in this graph, which I do believe dictates the course of humanity, I find a sense of relief and peace that when things get worse, they are bound to get better, and over time, things get better permanently, even if they do get worse for times. This is true for both, society at a large scale over centuries, and for human beings over their lifespans (on average). I hope you too can find solace in these facts and not let yourself be blinded by radicalism: find the stabilizing value in societal concepts, and stay away from the poisons that radicalize each extreme. Over time, the stabilizing value will trend upwards towards “goodness”, without you racking your brain over in which direction your life or your country is going: just be a factor in it going up and to the right over time and the rest of humanity will sort itself out.
