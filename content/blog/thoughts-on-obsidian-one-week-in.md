I've been strangely passionate about Obsidian for a week. This post was meant to be much shorter, but it is what it is.

I've been on the lookout for a note-taking tool for over a decade now. I kept switching between Google Docs, Google Sheets (paged, pageless...), Google Slides, Notion. Nothing has ever felt right -- it has always been a case of me forcing the tool to make it work for me, not having the tool work effortlessly for the task I have it for. An esoteric analogy that comes to mind is Gordon Ramsay's habitude "let the knife do the work" while filleting a fish as finding the right tool for the job. Everything I have used has been like kind of forcing a spoon edge to act as a makeshift knife.

Obsidian came across as a blip on my radar a couple years ago, but I was too adapted to making the Google workspace work that I didn't really consider attaching another whole dependency to my stack. Recently, I came across Obsidian again. I thought hey, what the heck, let me give it a shot -- downloaded it, the interface seemed incredibly annoying, and made me want to shut it down.

That night, I thought I would give it a fair shake and watched a 20 minute Youtube tutorial on it; and god damn it became my favourite piece of personal tooling over the past week (Well, excluding Claude Code, but that has a LOT going for it).

The simple idea of representing knowledge as a graph and creating links to things you know has been well explored among wiki-esque pages, but it had always been a second class citizen in note taking apps. Here, tangents and backlinks are first class citizens. You write something, another idea pops in your head: it is seamless to write it down and then go back to what you were doing earlier. Your thoughts don't have to follow strict hierarchical layering; structure will arrive naturally as your knowledge graph compounds.

![A folder tree beside a graph of the same five notes. In the tree, Habits and Sleep sit in different folders with no way to connect them; in the graph, they link directly.](/blog/obsidian/folders-vs-links.svg "Folders give every note one home. Links let a note sit wherever it relates.")

However, Obsidian is for individuals. It is for YOUR personal knowledge graph. It would not work as a collaborative tool without heavily changing the opinions it is built on, so if you're looking for a knowledge-base for your team, I personally don't recommend it. It also doesn't have cloud sync, but this is nothing a small auto-sync script with github and/or google drive can't fix.

I already even contributed a couple plugins and a theme to the app's community marketplace, which I usually won't do until I have heavily bought into a product.

See, Obsidian feels like old-school technology. It's solid, it's seamless and it works, they don't care about marketing or selling you something every 5 minutes (or at all, actually). The product is what the product is. If you like it, use it; if you don't, uninstall it. And I want to subscribe to that philosophy just for now. Much of LinkedIn is often used for marketing or selling something (yourself, your idea, your company, and LinkedIn constantly selling their own services to you): but this post is just for my current gushing love towards Obsidian. It was everything I ever wanted from my note-taking app (LaTeX works!!!!!) and it's everything I never even knew I wanted.

The best time to have picked up Obsidian would've been when it came up on my radar. The second-best time is now. And since I'm in for a dime, I'm in for a dollar. I hope I share this same opinion in a few months' time.
