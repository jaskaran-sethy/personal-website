I've had this thought for a while now, and it is heavily counter-cultural, something I try to avoid since I've always been a radical public opinion contrarian -- something I've been trying to work on; and yet, I find myself exactly as the title of this suggests -- willing to forgive myself for deviant thoughts and express them regardless.

There has been a certain push in society towards self-acceptance and healing, and allowing yourself to forgive yourself for your mistakes. This is actually extremely useful advice for a lot of people, nothing productive comes from wallowing in guilt. However, I really do need to argue, I do not think this is how the majority of the people on the planet are. Perhaps it may just be due to how abrasive I find it (a la Confirmation Bias) , but I notice far more people erring on the exact opposite end of this: they are almost TOO willing to forgive themselves for their wrongdoings.

You should feel bad when you do something wrong and it affects other people badly. Letting that persist for too long is where the issue lies for the first category of people, but letting it go immediately is where the second category live. It is the weird omnipresence of this second kind of personality, where they are enjoying the fruits of the advice given to the first category of people as generic advice to be worse human beings. There can often be context around this particular shortcoming that may justify it (like damn, your life really IS going terribly), but that tends to seldom be the case.

It is far too easy to forgive yourself and move on from things. That is not supposed to be the hard part. Knowing you are allowed to do that is (again, for the first category of people). I find people wronging something and forgetting about it one hour later, never to reflect or review on their behavior ever again. The only instances of guilt they feel are in not having taken action when they themselves were wronged, not ever registering the instances where they personally did wrong by others. In general, introspection and improvement upon oneself for these individuals seems to happen only about things that inflect them -- it's never about reflection as a byproduct of the damage they cause.

It's easy to forgive yourself to make that one purchase, justify saying you rarely do anything for yourself (don't actually use brain power to think when you did do something excessively self-indulgent though, you might find its instance not even a week ago -- that would break the narrative, of course). It's easy to act badly towards someone and blame it towards your emotions, of course you reacted strongly -- you were volatile due to how bad your day has been and how much your head hurts. Of course that bank clerk was wasting your time and deserved to be bitched about -- you've had a tough day after lounging around too long and dealing with your quirky coworker or boss as if they were the biggest inconvenience on planet earth. None of these are your personal failings, you simply deserve to forgive yourself for this small luxury you allow yourself and you simply "deserve" to move on. I would not argue these are deliberate choices being made by category 2 people every time -- just patterns they have fallen into they now subconsciously refuse to get out of, since leaving them would make their life worse in the short term as they learn to cope.

Genuine self-forgiveness is an occurrence that can only happen once you truly explore the damage you have done or are doing, not something to have glossed over. The grace you give yourself should be earned, something you have reflected on and strived to do better (and will actually act on) -- something you have striven to take accountability for, not pretty white lies whose only impact on you is the waste of brain power you needed to process and ignore them in the same instant of time. Do not let guilt consume you (especially when things cannot be fixed and are out of your control), but not letting guilt be the tool of the human psyche it is meant to be is also a deference from being human.\
And then, once you have actually gone through this process of exploring the damage, please let yourself be the category 1 person and actually learn to forgive yourself instead of mulling over it forever.

Like all things in life, people tend to act as lunatics riding on the bob of a swinging pendulum instead of stable entities with balanced outlooks and mentalities. In any binary description of populace: Don't be a category 1 or a category 2, be somewhere in the middle.

And yet again, my concluding paragraph is one of "don't be like the average person"; but I think that pertains true to any venue of life where you actually want to achieve something. Even quick self-forgiveness is a venue towards psychopathy, something that does really well in achieving extraordinary career success. And yet, perhaps, there is something to being like the average person but not the median person -- if it so happens that humanity tends to divide itself into two camps on any topic, the average person would be one that subscribes to both philosophies partially even if the median person needs to necessarily belong to one or the other. That balance itself might be worth striving for -- if anything but to live an actually fulfilling life.

I write this, of course, with an understanding that such a topic can stand to be deeply ironical. I write to find balance in achieving peace with your own wrongdoings, letting them neither fest too long nor too little to even be a blip on your mental radar. And yet, throughout this article, I have been a binary actor myself -- ridiculing a second category of people for their shortcomings while giving a lot of grace to the first category of people. Perhaps because the first category of people damage themselves, whereas the second category damage everyone but themselves. More than that, this article is also partially written for me, I am also a person that can get into the habit of over-forgiving myself too easily. That does not make me, or any category 2 person, inherently evil -- this behavior can be driven to change, and that is what I am advocating for; just a different perspective from the widespread "Forgive yourself for that thing you did." Do not use the language of healing and pop therapy to avoid accountability.

And of course, many people would argue that guilt is not a motivator for accountability and action and rather serves as a pure impediment. I find this argument a little lacking and non-human, guilt is an emotion humans experience. The existence of unproductive guilt doesn't justify treating all guilt as unproductive: using your emotions as tools to better yourself is one of the best things you can do.

Be kind to yourself, but also be kind to others. Be willing to confront the wrongs you do. Move on, but not without genuine self reflection and accountability.
